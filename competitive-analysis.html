01 · Competitive analysis

Who else
coaches the mix?

I compared my rekordbox companion app with 7 DJ set-prep and coaching tools across 12 dimensions. One tool beats it at live coaching, one is the biggest threat, and the comparison produced 5 changes to the roadmap.

Compared DeckSpin · SetFlow · Coach DJ Pro · Mixed In Key 11 · DJ.Studio · rekordbox 7 · Lexicon Method Public product pages, changelogs, pricing, and DeckSpin's shipped JS bundle, with every claim sourced Date Nov 2026
02 · Summary

The whole loop is the edge. Move confirmation is the gap.

No tool I reviewed does all three steps: order a full set by BPM, key, and energy; write the transition moves into rekordbox as cues; and coach those moves on the DJ's own controller. Every competitor covers one or two of the three.

What I found

  • DeckSpin beats Move Coach on live coaching today. It reads a DDJ-FLX4 over Web MIDI, pauses until you fix a wrong control, and grades every move. My MIDI listener reads the controls but doesn't check them yet.
  • SetFlow is the biggest threat. It is an established, paid product with set ordering, mix points, and an on-screen tutor. It doesn't write new cues to rekordbox or read a real controller, at least not yet.
  • Setup is my weakest point. Competitors run in a browser. Mine needs Python and a local server.

04 · The competitors

Positioning, strengths and weaknesses

DeckSpin Closest on coaching

A Lovable-built browser prototype titled "Deck School." You drop in two songs, check the downbeat by ear, and pick one of 7 lessons, from a hard cut to a loop roll. Then you play it as a rhythm game on a 3D FLX4 or your real one over Web MIDI. Strong: zero install, move confirmation, polish. Weak: two songs at a time, no rekordbox link, no pricing or company shown.

SetFlow Biggest threat

A paid browser set builder for "weekend and working DJs," shipping several releases a week. It orders full sets, shows where to start the mix and swap the bass, and its DJ Tutor shows each transition on an on-screen controller. Weak: it doesn't write new cues to rekordbox "yet," doesn't read a controller, and puts the tutor behind its Pro plan.

Coach DJ Pro

A local desktop app that scores a recorded set's transitions on 7 axes and tracks progress. Its paid Live Assistant suggests the next track. Weak: feedback comes after the set, from audio, with no controller guidance.

Mixed In Key 11

The standard for key detection and the 1–10 Energy Level, which my ordering is calibrated to. It adds up to 8 cues per track. Weak: its cues mark structure, not moves, and there is no set builder or coaching.

DJ.Studio

A timeline DAW for recorded mixes. v5.0 added phrase detection and export with cue points. Weak: it is built for offline mixes, has no coaching, and costs $99 or more.

rekordbox 7 and Lexicon

rekordbox's CUE Analysis auto-places cues from phrase analysis and learns from your own cueing, and the FLX4's Smart Fader automates the bass swap. Lexicon generates template cues and suggests what to play next. Weak: neither one orders a set by BPM and key together or coaches moves.

05 · Where it wins and loses

An honest scorecard

Wins

  • The only tool that orders the set, writes the moves into rekordbox, and coaches them on the controller.
  • Cues say what to do (BASS SWAP, FILTER, a way out every 16 bars) and are paired across both tracks.
  • The prep stays in rekordbox for the gig, without the app open.
  • A legally sourced 300-track practice catalog in 5 levels.
  • Free and local.

Losses

  • No move confirmation yet, while DeckSpin ships it.
  • Needs Python and a local server, while competitors run in a browser.
  • Less polish and engagement than DeckSpin's game loop.
  • No tester data yet, while SetFlow publishes usage numbers.
  • rekordbox and FLX4 only, and it can't analyze streamed tracks.
06 · What I'd change

5 product insights

1. Ship move confirmation Highest priority

The MIDI listener already stores the DJ's real fader and knob positions. Comparing them with the coach's target, and pausing on a wrong move the way DeckSpin does, turns the core claim from a design into a feature and powers the "moves completed on time" metric.

2. Sell the loop, not "the only coach"

"Order your set, get the moves written onto your rekordbox waveforms, then practice them on your own controller." DeckSpin coaches and SetFlow tutors, but neither leaves you with a prepped library.

3. Make Move Coach run in the browser

Web MIDI and Web Audio in Chrome are enough for live coaching, as both competitors show. Keep Python for the rekordbox read and write only, and let the hosted demo accept a real FLX4.

4. Check the downbeat before writing cues

Borrow DeckSpin's "does the high click land on the one?" step. Every cue depends on bar 1, so this targets the biggest accuracy risk and the 70% cue-keep goal.

5. Benchmark against rekordbox's own cues and watch SetFlow

Compare cue keep rates against rekordbox CUE Analysis and Mixed In Key. If SetFlow ships cue write-back and controller input, lead with the paired action cues and the cue light.

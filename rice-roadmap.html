02 · Strategy

A prepped set and a coach on the controller

Vision: a beginner DJ can take their own playlist and walk into their first gig with a set that's already prepped and a coach on the controller.

Who it serves

Student DJs at UT on rekordbox and a beginner controller like the DDJ-FLX4. There are two segments: Preppers, who own local files and want a set ordered and cued, and Learners, who mostly stream and need music to practice on and someone to show them the moves.

The wedge

It orders the set by BPM and key together, which rekordbox can't do. It places named cues that say what move to make, and Move Coach prompts each move on the controller, timed to the DJ's own tracks. The live, MIDI-driven version is still a prototype whose FLX4 mapping hasn't been verified. Today that kind of coaching mostly comes from a paid lesson.

Versus the alternatives

  • rekordbox tutorials are generic and not tied to the DJ's own tracks.
  • Video courses keep learning separate from playing a real set.
  • Coach DJ Pro, the closest competitor, scores a set after it's played. Move Coach coaches during the set.

What the scoring surfaced

  • Most student DJs stream, and the app can't analyze streamed audio. So the core prep loop reaches only the Preppers, and it scores mid-table even though its impact is the highest.
  • The practice catalog and Move Coach Practice mode work without local files, so they reach everyone and score highest. That matches the October pivot that turned the app into a learning tool.

Where I overrode the score

  • Metrics instrumentation (#7) moves up into R1. Without it, none of the design doc's success metrics can be measured.
  • The Countdown Light (#12, #13) stays in R3 despite low scores. It's the product's hardware track, gated on the app having real users first, and the PCB only starts if the bench build passes.
  • Going from a 20-DJ to a 5-DJ cohort shrank every score but kept the order, the releases and these overrides the same. With 5 DJs, a single tester moves Reach by 20% or more, so week-1 interviews will matter more than any other input. The PCB build dropped to 2 units, which makes perfboard a real alternative to a custom PCB.
  • Genre bridging (#14) is out of every release. Spotify integration was cut from scope in October, so it isn't scored.
04 · Releases

Earn trust, then coach, then build hardware

Each release has a gate that must pass before the next one starts. The metrics are the design doc's targets, measured against a baseline from week-1 interviews.

R1 · Trust and measurement ~3 pw est.

Safe re-import (#2), the benchmark (#4), metrics instrumentation (#7) and one-command setup (#9), on top of the shipped prep loop, practice catalog and Practice mode.

Targets: 5 active testers (with only 2 of 5 assumed to own files, that means recruiting testers who own files or counting sets prepped from catalog tracks; Maria to decide), prep time cut by at least 50%, at least 70% of cues kept, and 0 hand-set cues lost.

Gate: the re-import merge question is answered.

R2 · Coach the live set ~3.5 pw est.

Verify the prototype's FLX4 MIDI mapping on a real controller and finish Live Move Coach (#6), confirm moves through MIDI with an on-time score (#5), and add the lesson progress path (#8).

Targets: the share of cued moves made on time improves from a tester's first session to their third (the exact target comes from the R2 baseline), and 4 of 5 testers would use it again.

Gate: the FLX4 mapping is verified, and the timing engine stays in sync through tempo nudges.

R3 · Take it to the booth ~3–6 pw est.

The Countdown Light bench build and serial bridge (#12) and a second controller map (#11). The PCB and 2-unit build (#13) only happen if the bench build passes, and only if a PCB beats hand-wired perfboard for 2 units.

Targets: the ring stays in step with a metronome at several tempos, testers make more moves on time with the light than without it, and at least 1 tester on another controller completes a coached set.

Not planned: genre bridging and streamed-audio analysis.

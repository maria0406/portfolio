02 · Problem

Prepping a set takes skill

The biggest gap I've found, for myself and for other people getting into DJing, is that you don't know where to start. You can learn what every control on the deck does, but once you load a song you don't know what to move, when to move it, or what you're listening for.

Why it's hard

  • Most beginners watch a couple of videos and try to pick it up from there, using someone else's songs.
  • One of the first rules you learn is to mix songs with compatible keys and BPMs, but rekordbox only sorts by one or the other.
  • Even with the right songs, mixing by feel is hard without a music background, because you don't know what to listen for, like a build-up gaining speed before the drop.
03 · Who it's for

Student DJs at UT on beginner controllers

They play rekordbox on controllers like the Pioneer DDJ-FLX4 and want a set they can actually play, plus a way to learn the moves on their own tracks.

04 · What it does

Order the set, mark every transition, then teach the moves

Orders the set

Reads a playlist straight from the local rekordbox library and analyzes each track's tempo, beat grid, key, phrases and energy. Then it orders the tracks by BPM and key together, with energy shaped to a set arc.

Marks every transition

Places named, color-coded cues: MIX IN, MIX OUT, BASS SWAP, FILTER, DROP and an 8-bar safety loop. They export back to rekordbox as XML. On a 12-track test set, all 12 tracks imported with every cue at the exact time and name.

Move Coach

An animated controller in the FLX4's layout shows which knob or fader to move, which way and when, timed to the bars and phrases of the actual song. A single timing engine drives both the animation and an in-browser audio mix, so the demo you watch always matches what you hear.

Graded practice library

300 Creative Commons house tracks from 128 artists, scored on how hard they are to mix (intro and outro length, tempo stability, energy changes). They're split into 5 levels and paired into lessons, from "your first blend" to "hard transitions".

05 · How I built it

Local-first, legally sourced, tested against rekordbox itself

Stack

Python with FastAPI, librosa for audio analysis and pyrekordbox to read the rekordbox database. The front end is vanilla JavaScript, SVG and the Web Audio API. Everything runs locally, so music never leaves the DJ's laptop.

Research first

I compared where a learning tool could legally get DJ-ready audio: Creative Commons libraries, research datasets, streaming partner programs and royalty-free catalogs. Most major catalogs forbid analyzing or redistributing their tracks, so I built on Creative Commons netlabel releases from the Internet Archive.

Tested against the real thing

I imported the generated cues into rekordbox 7 and checked them against its database, then fixed the problems that turned up. A 12-track set now imports with every cue at the exact time and name.

06 · Hardware extension

Beat-Synced Cue Light

A small ESP32-powered LED ring that sits next to the DJ controller and turns the set plan into light, so a DJ can keep their eyes on the decks instead of the laptop.

On the beat

The ring pulses with every beat, stronger on the first beat of each bar, in time with the track that's playing.

Follows the music

Its color and brightness follow the track's energy and section: calmer in a breakdown, fuller at a drop.

Counts down to the next move

Eight bars before a cue (MIX OUT, BASS SWAP, FAKE DROP…) an amber arc starts shrinking around the ring, then flashes the cue's color at the moment to act.

How it works

  • The laptop app already knows each track's beat positions, energy curve and cue times. It streams a compact timing message to the ESP32 over USB serial about 10 times a second: tempo, time to the next beat, position in the bar, energy, and the next cue with its countdown.
  • The ESP32 runs its own beat clock between messages, so the light stays smooth and on time even if a message arrives late. It renders effects at 60 frames per second with the FastLED library.
  • The same timing engine that drives the on-screen Move Coach also drives the light. In practice mode it follows the app's own audio playback; during a live set it follows the controller's button presses.
